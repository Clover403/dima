**1. The Logic Behind the Texts & The Scroll-Down Narrative**

As I mentioned in our initial documentation, the Home page is designed to touch on a little bit of everything. To avoid making the page tedious and to prevent the audience from losing interest, it is crucial not to reveal everything at once. Instead, we are offering just enough of a "teaser" to capture their attention and guide them to the dedicated tabs.

Because this page combines Open Interactivity and an Immersive Scroll-Down Experience, the texts follow a strict narrative sequence. There is a precise logic to their order (e.g., reading the "Modelo" before the "Proceso"). The order in which I present the texts below is the exact order they must appear on the site.

**2. The Master Animation Concept (The Macro-Economy)**

Since the text already provides the storytelling, the question is: What exactly are we going to animate to visually match this narrative?

Because the texts speak broadly about the economy, development, credit, and productivity, we do not need a customized illustration sitting next to each specific paragraph. Instead, we want one continuous, morphing animation of the main economic sectors, perfectly blending together as the user scrolls.

* **The Vision:** Think of the terminal-industries.com 3D truck animation. Now, imagine that exact fluid transformation, but using our hand-drawn architectural illustration style.


* **The Sequence:** Imagine an animation of a building being constructed from scratch (from excavation to completion). As the user scrolls, that building seamlessly transforms into the agro-industrial sector (large tractors working the fields, crops growing). Then, it morphs into the energy sector, and so on.


* **The Advantage:** You already did something similar in the "Nosotros" tab where shapes morph into different forms. Applying this continuous morphing logic to the Home page means the animation speaks for itself about "growth and productivity," serving as the perfect visual anchor for the entire page.



(The metaphorical illustrations currently on the Home page are beautiful, but they are much better suited for the "Modelo" tab, where we talk specifically about the nature of credit and need abstract concepts rather than realistic objects).
